I revel in problems that don't have a shape yet. At Oak I conceived and built the Oak AI Plugin that puts the open curriculum inside AI assistants such as ChatGPT and Claude.

My joy of the unknown began with physics. As a published scientist studying the early Universe, I built insight; validated and refined it against reality; and moved knowledge forward. As an innovator and technologist I went further: understanding systems is gratifying; learning where to make small changes for outsized impact is sublime, and useful.

In the spring of 2020 Covid was causing schools to close. Oak started as an emergency online classroom to ensure access to education: we served over 160 million lessons, with 2.5 million pupils using Oak in one week at the peak. Impact scaled and evolved, and now Oak is the UK's official curriculum body, a public service used by over 200,000 teachers. It saves them four hours a week, and is used most where disadvantage is highest. I joined to get the first teacher-facing service over the line. I stayed to shape what a complete, openly licensed, digital-native, AI-ready curriculum becomes in other people's hands: what they build with it, and the impact that creates.

The hardest call was arguing to rebuild the emergency platform while it was still what kept lessons online. Four years on, everything stands on the platform that replaced it.

The last few years have been driven by one idea: Oak's curriculum should be wherever teachers already are, including inside the AI assistants they use. Alongside the plugin, I built a way of working: the Practice is an agentic software engineering framework for building innovative products, at pace, without sacrificing quality. It learns as it goes, preserving critical institutional knowledge ignored and lost in many AI systems. Oak's Open Curriculum Ecosystem, the home of the plugin, has been built entirely with the Practice.

Before Oak: a consultancy of my own, the FT Web App, quality and delivery practice and tools for British Airways and the UK Passport Office, HP Labs, physics research, teaching, and tour guiding. Each was the same move at a different scale: see where the system gives, build what moves it, hand it on, and go looking for the next problem that has no shape yet.

The more I learn, the bigger the questions get.
