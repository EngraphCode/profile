# Truth and attribution

This file is loaded at step 4 and used through steps 6 and 8. It defines the fact ledger, the four
attribution classes and the verbs each allows, how figures, awards and organisational results are
handled, confidentiality, the interview-proof test, and honesty about assistance.

## The fact ledger

Every particular that will appear in an answer is entered in the ledger before it is drafted:
names of things, roles of people, dates, figures, titles, products, decisions, outcomes. The
ledger template is [assets/fact-ledger.md](../assets/fact-ledger.md). Each row carries:

- the particular, as it will appear;
- its source: the writer's words (verbatim, with the date captured), a document, a public page;
- its status: confirmed by the writer; checkable against a named source; unconfirmed;
- its attribution class (below), for claims about what the writer did;
- the answer or answers it appears in.

The rule at proof (step 6, pass 3) and at finalising (step 8): nothing is in an answer that is
not in the ledger as confirmed or checkable. An unconfirmed particular is removed or made vague
enough to be true ("several", "a six-figure sum") until the writer confirms it.

You, the agent, never add a row from your own knowledge. If you know the organisation's
published figure, you may ask the writer whether they want to use it and where it is published;
the writer confirms, and the source is the publication.

## The four attribution classes

Every claim about what the writer did belongs to one class. The class fixes the verbs the answer
may use.

| Class       | Meaning                                                                 | Verbs allowed                                                                | Verbs not allowed                               |
| ----------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Decided     | The writer made the call and it was adopted                             | decided, chose, set, stopped, hired, argued for and won, designed, built      | none withheld; the verb must still match the act |
| Influenced  | The writer proposed, argued or shaped; someone else decided             | proposed, argued for, made the case, recommended, persuaded, shaped           | decided, led the decision                        |
| Contributed | The writer did part of the work under someone else's direction           | built, wrote, ran, tested, delivered (the part), worked on, paired on         | led, designed (the whole), owned                 |
| Observed    | The writer was present or in the team but did not do the thing          | saw, learned from, was part of the team that                                  | any first-person act verb for the thing itself   |

Two rules follow.

- **The verb matches the class.** "I stopped the rebuild" needs Decided. "I made the case for
  stopping and the director decided" is Influenced, stated exactly, and reads as stronger than an
  unsupported "led" because the reader can see who decided.
- **Credit sits beside the claim.** "I chose the integration; the team built it; the director
  signed off each release" gives three owners to three claims. An assessor trusts an
  answer that knows who did what. "As part of a group" is honest when the group decided
  together and the writer's own part cannot be separated; where it can, name it.

The attribution question in [elicitation.md](elicitation.md), "if the other people in the room
read this, what would they say you did?", settles the class. The writer's answer is recorded,
and the draft is held to it.

## Organisational results

A result the organisation publishes (users served, money saved, an audit passed) is usable as
context and as the outcome of the writer's act only where the writer's act connects to it in the
ledger. "The service is used by 40,000 residents" is context. "Availability held at 99.95% over
the year after the provider change I delivered" connects the figure to the act. "Our organisation
won an award" with no connection is noise, and a strict assessor reads it as borrowed.

Figures go in the form and period in which they were published or measured, and the ledger
holds the source. Awards and recognitions go in the status the awarding body's own record
shows: a nomination is a nomination, a shortlist is a shortlist. A remembered win with no record
is named to the writer as unconfirmed and left out.

## Confidentiality

Roles, not names, for other people: "the finance director", "a senior engineer on my team".
Clients and partners by kind where the relationship is not public: "a regulated client". Figures
that are commercially or personally sensitive in their form only: "a six-figure contract", with
the real figure in the ledger. Nothing from a current employer that the employer would not want
read by a competitor; when in doubt, the writer asks the employer or leaves it out. The ledger is
the writer's private document and is never submitted.

## The interview-proof test

Every sentence in a sift answer is a sentence an interviewer may pick up: "tell me more about
that". For each sentence, the writer should be able to go two levels deeper from memory: the
detail behind it, and the detail behind that. A sentence the writer cannot deepen is cut or
softened before submission, whatever it does for the score. The ledger, kept, is the interview
preparation.

## Honesty about assistance

The common employer statement allows assistance and asks that the answers be true to the
applicant's experience and in the applicant's own voice, and warns that answers which could be
anyone's, or read as a machine's, are marked down.
Some employers ask applicants to confirm the answers are their own work; a few forbid assistance
altogether. Follow the employer's statement. The division of labour in this skill exists so that
the answers meet the common statement's two tests by construction: the facts are the writer's,
the sentences are the writer's or rewritten by the writer from their own captured words, and the
agent's part is questions, order, cuts and checks. "Write me an answer for this question" fails
both tests and the agent declines it. If a form asks whether assistance was used, the writer
answers truthfully; this skill gives them a true answer to give.
