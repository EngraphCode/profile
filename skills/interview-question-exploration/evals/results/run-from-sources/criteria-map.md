### The questions, verbatim

Each is answered in 250 words or fewer (a word limit, not characters).

1. Question: Tell us about a time you stopped or redirected a piece of work your team had
   already invested in. How did you reach the decision, how did you take the people who had
   built it with you, and what did the freed capacity do?
   Limit: 250 words.
2. Question: Tell us about a service you were responsible for failed the people who depend on
   it. What did you do in the first days, what did you change afterwards, and what of that
   change is still in place?
   Limit: 250 words.
3. Question: Q3, Tell us about introducing a new way of working to a team that did not want
   it. What was the resistance, how did you work with it, and what is the practice's state now?
   Limit: 250 words.
4. Question: Tell us about a time a budget or headcount reduction landed on your area. What did
   you choose to protect, what did you drop, and how did you explain that to the people it
   affected?
   Limit: 250 words.

### The published criteria, verbatim

Essential:

1. Leading a multidisciplinary digital team through change, with evidence of decisions that
   shaped the team.
2. Running live public-facing services to a standard: availability, accessibility and data
   protection.
3. Prioritising within a fixed and reducing budget, and explaining the choices to the people
   affected.
4. Improving how a team works, with evidence that the improvement lasted.
5. Learning from service failures, and changing the service rather than the explanation.
6. Working with frontline staff and residents as sources of evidence for decisions.
7. Developing people: coaching, delegation and succession.

Desirable:

8. Working with suppliers and other councils on shared services.

### The marking scheme

Published: "Each is answered in 250 words or fewer and scored on its own, one to five, by the
person who wrote it." Applications are sifted anonymously; the applicant's name and CV are hidden
from the people scoring. Nothing else is published, so the default rubric in
`references/rubric.md` applies, and this run says so.

## The choice per question

### Q1: E2, the Westmere rebuild stopped (June 2023)

- Answers every part? (a) yes, sourced; (d) partly, sourced (the redirection and the figure);
  (b) and (c) are not in any source, so unknown rather than failed.
- The writer decided the central thing? S1's verb is "stopped", which signals the Decided
  class; the class is the writer's to set at the provenance pass. Open.
- The counterpart is real and can appear? "The team" that had built four months of work exists
  in the source; nobody in it appears. Open.
- The outcome is of the counted kind? Yes: a figure for what the redirected capacity delivered,
  with its period ("from eleven days to six by spring 2024").
- Something persists? Not stated. Open.
- Alternative in the bank: none. Known holes: elements 3, 4, 6; the evidence and reasoning in
  element 2; parts (b) and (c). Expect the scaffold to score below three until parts (b) and (c)
  are answered.

### Q2: E3, the Westmere repairs-reporting outage (February 2024)

- Answers every part? (a) yes, sourced; (c) yes, sourced (three changes); (b) and (d) are not
  in any source.
- The writer decided the central thing? S1 says "led the response" and "introduced". The
  question's premise, "a service you were responsible for", is not stated by the source; open.
- The counterpart? The residents who could not report repairs, whoever was paged, any supplier:
  none appears. Open.
- The outcome of the counted kind? No service result after the changes is sourced; the counted
  outcome is wholly an ask.
- Persists? The three changes are stated as introduced (2024); their state now is not. Open.
- A risk to name to the writer: "over a storm weekend" reads as context; left alone it reads
  as the weather's fault, which is the one-star behaviour for this question. The ask for the
  cause found is where that is settled.
- Alternative: none (E8 is a standard held, not a failure). Known holes: elements 2 (the first
  days), 3, 4, 5, 6; parts (b) and (d). Expect the scaffold to score below three.

### Q3: E5 proposed, the Eastgate replay gate (autumn 2021); E1 the alternative

- The question asks for a team that did not want the practice. Neither source states resistance
  to either candidate. This is said plainly here and in the scaffold; nothing is inferred from
  S2's "nobody on the team had run anything through a spike like it" or "I was the person most
  worried", which describe inexperience and worry, not resistance.
- E5 holds the most of any candidate: the particular, the method and a reason, the outcome in
  both sources, what persists in both sources, part of the cost. It is proposed for that reason
  and for spreading the set across two organisations (Q1, Q2 and Q4 are all Westmere).
- E1 holds the practice, the team's composition and a figure, and nothing else.
- If the writer says nobody resisted the replay gate, E5 cannot carry Q3 and the choice moves to
  E1; if nobody resisted the definition of done either, the honest options are the nearest true
  episode written plainly, which will score about three, or the bank interview on criterion 4
  and leading change. Rule 3 of the skill: never dress a different kind of episode in the
  criterion's words. E5 was written in 2023 for "delivering under pressure"; the scaffold lays
  its sentences as they are and asks, it does not re-dress them.
- The practice's state now: both sources assert it (S2 in 2023, S1 in 2024) after the writer
  left the museum in 2022. How the writer knows is an ask.
- Known holes: part (b) wholly, part (c) with it; element 3 for the practice (the head of
  digital appears on the build decision, not on the gate); element 6's reflection; the method's
  reason over an alternative. Expect the scaffold to score below three for this question.
