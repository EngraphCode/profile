Question 2. Tell us about a service you were responsible for failed the people who depend on it. What did you do in the first days, what did you change afterwards, and what of that change is still in place?

In February 2024 the repairs-reporting service I ran at Westmere County Council was down for
fourteen hours over a storm weekend. The housing office manager rang me on Sunday: three people
on phones instead of visits, and nothing to tell residents. The engineer restored it, and I
posted a notice promising phone reports would be honoured in order. On Monday I sat in the
housing office; residents told us they assumed the council had ignored them. All 214 phone
reports were logged by Tuesday evening, and the urgent ones were visited inside our
forty-eight-hour promise. The filing process had stuck on a malformed photo upload, and its
alert went to a mailbox nobody read at weekends. I had known for months that mailbox was weak
and had put it behind other work. I introduced a paid weekend on-call rota, validation that
rejects a bad upload, and a check that submits a test report and pages. The rota's money came
from a contractor line I held; the director of resident services signed it off. The validation
and the check cost the engineer two weeks meant for the benefits form's accessibility fixes; I
told its service manager before he started, and she agreed a month's delay because the audit
re-test was in June. The check has caught two faults since, both fixed before a resident
noticed. All three are still in place, and the housing office manager now gets the same page
we do. She asked for it; I should have offered.
