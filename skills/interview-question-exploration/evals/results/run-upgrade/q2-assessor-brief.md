# Assessor brief, as given to the fresh reader (question 2, round 1, 2026-10-21)

Given to a fresh agent as its whole prompt, with nothing else: not the ledger, not the
diagnosis, not what the writer intends the reader to believe.

---

You are an assessor at an English borough council, scoring the anonymised sift for the role of
Head of Digital Service in the council's housing and benefits directorate. You have no CV, no
name and no other answers from this applicant. You are reading a batch of answers to the same
question in one sitting, each once, at an assessor's pace, as plain text with the question above
it.

The question, verbatim:

> Tell us about a service you were responsible for failed the people who depend on it. What
> did you do in the first days, what did you change afterwards, and what of that change is still
> in place?

The criterion this question tests, verbatim from the published criteria:

> Learning from service failures, and changing the service rather than the explanation.

The marking scheme:

Score from 1 to 5. One is a basically meaningless answer: generic, off the question, could be
anyone's. Three is a competent answer: a specific real example, the applicant's own actions
clear, every part of the question answered, an outcome stated. Five is comprehensive and
excellent: everything in three, and the other people in the situation appear as people the
applicant read and responded to, the method is named and the reason for choosing it given where
the question asks how, the cost and any obstacles are honest and navigated, the outcome is
checkable and of the kind this criterion is about, the applicant states an honest judgement
about what worked and what they would refine, and their thinking reaches past the immediate
situation to what they changed so the bad thing cannot recur, so the good thing happens again,
and so other people can now handle it without them, each as an act rather than a belief about
themselves.

You are primed to mark down anything that could describe anyone's experience and to reward the
particular.

Read the answer below once. Then report, quoting exactly and describing effects rather than
proposing wording:

```text
Score: [1 to 5]
What I now believe about this applicant against the criterion: [two or three sentences]
The sentence that most earned the score: "[exact quote]"
The sentence that most cost it: "[exact quote]"
What was noise for me: [quoted sentences, each with why: nothing new, wrong altitude, unclear, not believed]
Where I skimmed or stopped, and why: [exact quote, or "read to the end"]
The one thing that would move this up a star: [one sentence]
```

The answer:

> In February 2024 the repairs-reporting service I was responsible for at Westmere County
> Council was down for fourteen hours over a storm weekend, with about three thousand attempts
> to open the form. The housing office manager rang me on the Sunday: three people on phones
> instead of visits, and nothing to tell residents. The engineer restored it that evening, me
> directing, and I posted a notice promising phone reports would be honoured in order. On the
> Monday morning I sat in the housing office; residents assumed the council had ignored them.
> All 214 phone reports were logged by the Tuesday evening, and the urgent ones, leaks near
> electrics and no heating, were visited inside the forty-eight hours our policy promises. The
> process that files each report had stuck on a malformed photo upload, and its alert had gone
> to a mailbox nobody read at weekends. I had known for months that mailbox was weak and had
> put it behind other work. I introduced a paid weekend on-call rota, validation that rejects a
> bad upload, and a check that submits a test report every five minutes and pages when it
> fails. The validation and the check took the engineer two weeks meant for the benefits form's
> accessibility fixes, which slipped a month. The check has caught two faults since, both fixed
> before a resident noticed. All three are still in place, and the manager now gets the same
> page we do. She asked for it; I should have offered.

## Round 2 (2026-10-21)

The same brief, word for word, given to a second fresh agent, with the answer below in place of
the round 1 answer (the 250-word answer, q2-current.md after turn 12).

> In February 2024 the repairs-reporting service I ran at Westmere County Council was down for
> fourteen hours over a storm weekend. The housing office manager rang me on Sunday: three
> people on phones instead of visits, and nothing to tell residents. The engineer restored it,
> and I posted a notice promising phone reports would be honoured in order. On Monday I sat in
> the housing office; residents told us they assumed the council had ignored them. All 214
> phone reports were logged by Tuesday evening, and the urgent ones were visited inside our
> forty-eight-hour promise. The filing process had stuck on a malformed photo upload, and its
> alert went to a mailbox nobody read at weekends. I had known for months that mailbox was weak
> and had put it behind other work. I introduced a paid weekend on-call rota, validation that
> rejects a bad upload, and a check that submits a test report and pages. The rota's money came
> from a contractor line I held; the director of resident services signed it off. The
> validation and the check cost the engineer two weeks meant for the benefits form's
> accessibility fixes; I told its service manager before he started, and she agreed a month's
> delay because the audit re-test was in June. The check has caught two faults since, both
> fixed before a resident noticed. All three are still in place, and the housing office manager
> now gets the same page we do. She asked for it; I should have offered.
