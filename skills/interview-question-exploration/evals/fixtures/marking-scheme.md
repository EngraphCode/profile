# Marking scheme for the invented Ashcombe sift

Invented for testing the skill, in the harness author's words; it mirrors no real employer's
scheme. Each question is scored one to five by its author. Each rung includes the rungs below
it; two and four sit between the ones described.

## Q1. Stopping or redirecting work under way

- **One star.** No specific piece of work; or the work was stopped by someone else; or the answer
  is about pressing on and rescuing it; or the decision is announced with no account of the
  people who had built it.
- **Three stars.** A specific piece of work, the evidence that led to the decision, the writer's
  own decision clear, something said about the team, and what happened to the capacity.
- **Five stars.** Everything in three. The evidence named and how the writer tested it before
  acting. The sunk cost acknowledged openly to the people who had spent it, their reaction
  described and answered. The freed capacity's result with a figure. An honest judgement on
  timing: what the writer would have stopped sooner, or later. Something that persists, such as a
  review point the team now uses before investing further.

## Q2. A service that failed its users

- **One star.** No specific failure, or a supplier's failure with the writer as spectator. Blame.
  A fix with no change to how the service is run.
- **Three stars.** A specific failure, who it affected and how, what the writer did in the first
  days, a change made afterwards, an outcome.
- **Five stars.** Everything in three. The users or the frontline staff told, and what they said.
  A cause found rather than a symptom patched. The change of the kind the criterion counts: a
  recurrence prevented, a recovery time met, a check that now runs. A judgement on what the
  writer had missed before the failure. The part that is still in place, named.

## Q3. Introducing a practice against resistance

- **One star.** The practice imposed. The resisters cast as the problem. No account of what they
  said. The practice's current state unknown.
- **Three stars.** A specific practice, the resistance described, what the writer did, the state
  now.
- **Five stars.** Everything in three. The resistance treated as information, with at least one
  thing the writer changed because of it. A method chosen over the obvious one, with the reason.
  Evidence that the practice is in use without the writer. A judgement on what the writer got
  wrong in the introduction.

## Q4. A budget or headcount reduction

- **One star.** The cut passed down evenly; nothing chosen. Or the answer is about arguing the
  cut away. Or the people affected are absent.
- **Three stars.** The size of the cut, what was protected and what was dropped, a reason for
  each, the people affected told.
- **Five stars.** Everything in three. The reasoning tied to the service's users rather than to
  the team's preferences. The people affected told before they heard it elsewhere, with what they
  asked and how the writer answered. The consequence of the dropped thing stated honestly. A
  judgement on what the writer would do differently. A mechanism that persists, such as a
  standing list of what is cut next.
