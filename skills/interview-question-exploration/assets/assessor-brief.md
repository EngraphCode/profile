# Assessor brief

Give this, filled in, to a fresh reader (a new agent context or a colleague who has not seen the
working papers) together with the answer. Give nothing else: not the ledger, not the worksheet,
not what the writer intends the reader to believe.

---

You are an assessor at [kind of organisation], scoring the anonymised sift for the role of
[role title]. You have no CV, no name and no other answers from this applicant. You are reading
a batch of answers to the same question in one sitting, each once, at an assessor's pace, as
plain text with the question above it.

The question, verbatim:

> [question]

The criterion this question tests, verbatim from the published criteria:

> [criterion]

The marking scheme:

[The employer's scheme if published; otherwise:] Score from 1 to 5. One is an empty answer:
generic, off the question, could be anyone's. Three is a sound answer: a specific real example,
the applicant's own actions clear, every part of the question answered, an outcome stated. Five
is a full answer that is excellent: everything in three, and the other people in the situation
appear as people the applicant read and responded to, the method is named and the reason for
choosing it given where the question asks how, the cost and any obstacles are honest and
navigated, the outcome is checkable and of the kind this criterion is about, the applicant
states an honest judgement about what worked and what they would refine, and something they
left in place is named (a check that runs, a runbook that is used, a person who now owns the
thing, a way of deciding the team adopted), each as an act rather than a belief about
themselves.

The employer has told applicants: [the employer's warning about generic or machine-written
answers, verbatim, or omit this paragraph]. You are primed to mark down anything that could
describe anyone's experience and to reward the particular.

Read the answer below once. Then report, quoting exactly and describing effects rather than
proposing wording:

```text
Score: [1 to 5]
What I now believe about this applicant against the criterion: [two or three sentences]
The sentence that most earned the score: "[exact quote]"
The sentence that most cost it: "[exact quote]"
What was noise for me: [quoted sentences, each with why: nothing new, wrong altitude, unclear, not believed]
Where I skimmed or stopped, and why: [exact quote, or "read to the end"]
The one thing that would move this up a star: [one sentence]
```

The answer:

> [the answer, exactly as it will be submitted]
