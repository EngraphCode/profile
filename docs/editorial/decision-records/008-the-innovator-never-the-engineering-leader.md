# EDR-008: The reader's decision on the professional surfaces — the innovator, never the engineering leader

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-10-11

## Context

On 10 October 2026 the LinkedIn Oak entries went through three rounds of readers in one day. The
seat's brief for the `audience-reader` cast the primary reader as "a head hunter retained for a
senior technology leadership search (head of engineering, CTO, technical director)" who "will look
for leadership of people and organisations". Every round then cured to that reader's objection:
a paragraph explaining Oak's levels as an org chart, a sentence saying who holds the budget and
the people, the reporting line on the card. The rounds grew the block from two cards to three and
from 2,454 to 2,986 characters, and the final reading reported the reader's doubt had turned
"from a suspicion into a certainty".

Jim's verdict on that round, 11 October: "Incorrect, and frankly the wording is still clumsy.
Step back. Step back more. ... Who are we trying to talk to, why? We are spending effort on this
writing, what do we need to accomplish with that effort? What is this all for?" His answer, in
substance: the profile exists to express his innovation and his broad interests and broad
capabilities; it is never a run-the-org engineering-leader profile. No route to a reader is
privileged: a search partner, a funder, a leader who reads it, a direct approach from someone
who read it, all count.

The frame that failed was inherited by analogy: "senior" and "head hunter" pattern-matched to
the conventional senior engineering search, where headcount and budget are the first screen and
a Principal title caps the candidate. On that axis the profile can only argue the title up, and
arguing a title up is justification (EDR-002). On the axis the readers actually decide on,
whether this is a person who originates, makes sense of unshaped problems, and shapes strategy
and systems with consequences that outlast him, the title is the smallest fact on the page and
the decisions and their consequences carry the level.

## Decision

1. **The decision axis.** Every reader of Jim's professional surfaces, whatever their role, is
   deciding whether this is a person who originates, makes sense of problems that have no shape
   yet, and shapes strategy and systems at scale, with the breadth of interest and capability to
   do it again somewhere else. No reader brief casts a hiring screen for a line role. A brief
   whose reader asks headcount, budget or reporting line as the first questions is mis-cast for
   this profile, and its findings are discarded with that reason.
2. **Level shows, it is never argued.** The job title is a fact and is carried plainly. The level
   of operation shows in decisions and their consequences: what was built and what was not, the
   call that was made and the cost accepted, what others now build on. An org chart, a reporting
   line or a statement of who holds the budget argues the title up and belongs on no card; the
   structured facts live in the entity graph and the CV's data where a machine or a verifier
   looks for them.
3. **A sentence of scope is welcome; an org chart is not.** Scope stated as fact in Jim's voice
   ("what gets built and what does not") filters the irrelevant approach and informs the relevant
   one. Explaining the organisation's levels does neither.
4. **Do not answer a mis-cast reader's question on the page.** Answering "who reported to you"
   in the open grades Jim on that reader's axis. If the axis is wrong, the question is not
   answered in copy; the conversation answers it.
5. **Any route.** Composition favours no route to a reader. The profile gives a relevant approach
   what it needs (coordinates, distinction, a reason to continue) and removes the generators of
   irrelevant ones (junior-reading titles, undifferentiated skills).

## Consequences

- `editorial-strategy.md` §Standing audiences carries the decision axis and the mis-cast test.
- `editorial-guidance.md` §Conveying a person carries "Level shows, it is never argued";
  `docs/editorial/author.md` §Standing decisions carries "The innovator, not the engineering leader".
- `write-for-the-reader` §1 and §5 require the reader's decision to be stated on this axis and the
  `audience-reader` brief to be built from it; a new gotcha names the mis-cast reader.
- The `audience-reader` template names the decision axis so a mis-cast brief contradicts it
  visibly.
- The Oak block on LinkedIn: the org-chart sentence and the reporting line leave the current
  card; the consulting cards are plain facts, not positioning essays.
- The seat's private material still supplies who the readers are for a given piece and what each
  trusts and doubts; this record fixes only the axis they decide on and what the copy never does.
