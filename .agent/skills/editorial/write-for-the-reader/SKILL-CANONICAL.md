  answer pasted as copy; copy cut to a character count; a card partitioned by a date seam.

# Write for the Reader

Copy that represents a person fails in a few recurring ways, and the standards that name every
fault already exist in `.agent/directives/editorial-strategy.md` (the reader's decision, the
attention path, section weight, evidence, the review sequence) and the `editorial-voice` skill
(the craft of a first-person voice). The faults recur because nothing fires the standards at
drafting time and the author is the first reader every time. This skill is the order of work that
fires them. The directives own the standards; this skill owns the sequence. The author's own
identity, readers, voice and standing decisions live in the host's author instance,
`.agent/directives/editorial-guidance.md` (its decision records under
`docs/editorial/decision-records/`); this skill never restates them and never names a person.

Its first worked lane, a complete LinkedIn profile rewritten and applied with its author present
between September and October 2026, produced the method below. The dated instances with the
author's own words are in the author instance, where they belong.

## 1. Before the first sentence: the reader's decision

Write it down and show it with the draft (`editorial-strategy.md` §Begin with the reader's
decision):

1. the primary reader by role, and any secondary reader that matters;
2. what that reader is deciding or doing, on the axis the author instance fixes, and what they
   are not deciding: a reader who screens for a role the author does not want, with that role's
   first questions, is the wrong reader for the piece, and answering those questions in copy
   grades the author on the wrong axis;
3. what they already believe, misunderstand or doubt at first contact;
4. the surface and the reading mode: a card, a collapsed preview, a scan, a full read, a search
   result, a machine extracting nouns (the surface skills, `linkedin-profile` and `cv`, hold what
   each surface shows and hides);
5. what must be newly clear when they finish.

A draft written before this exists is written for the material, and the material's shape leaks
into the prose. The reader's decision comes from the author instance and from conversation with
the author, never from a generic professional search: a generic list of audiences stood in for
the author's actual readers once, and three rounds of readers cured a draft to a reader the
author had never wanted.

## 2. The audience hops

The words cross a chain of audiences before they reach the reader: the author's words to the
seat; the seat's draft to the readers; the readers' beliefs back to the seat; the draft to the
author; the piece to its readers; the piece to the platform's collapsed forms. **Words never cross
a hop unchanged.** Every recurring failure of the first lane was a carry across one hop without
rewriting for the next audience:

- the author's answer in chat written into the piece as copy ("I wrote them for you, I did not
  write them for the audience");
- the sources' own sentences assembled into a draft (a "Frankenstein of earlier statement
  fragments");
- a reader's finding applied as a cure instead of weighed as information;
- a CV sentence moved into a profile field, or a settled list's shape leaking into prose;
- a headline made to carry a claim because search results show the headline alone: a surface's
  reach never licenses one field to do another field's job.

At every hop, name the audience on the other side and write for it.

## 3. The beliefs, then the moments

- Name the two or three things the reader must believe when they finish, in the words they would
  use to repeat them to someone else. That is the referability test, and it is the whole job. The
  belief applies to the whole as much as to each part: a profile read end to end must leave it.
- For each belief, choose the one moment that proves it, at the reader's altitude. A reader
  deciding at strategy altitude wants scope, the decision, the cost accepted and the consequence;
  mechanism is noise to them however true it is. The test for every candidate sentence: what does
  the named reader believe after it that they did not before? Nothing new, or new at the wrong
  altitude, and the sentence does not earn its place.
- Put attribution beside the claim it qualifies: what the author did, what others and agents did,
  what was not chosen, what it cost. A claim that survives inspection by the people who were there
  is the claim a verifier trusts.
- A high-impact claim is made once, prominently, never repeated across sections.

## 4. The prose

- Write continuous prose in one voice, by the `editorial-voice` skill: its registers, its
  pitfalls, its check. One job per paragraph; situation, decision, cost, consequence is a shape
  that works. A label is a first sentence only when it is the best first sentence, which it rarely
  is.
- The source's shape never dictates the prose's shape. A list of settled movements becomes
  paragraphs that list, and the reader meets a committee's digest instead of a person. Write from
  the beliefs; verify against the material afterwards.
- Never assemble. A draft stitched from the sources' own sentences reads as a dull litany of tell,
  not show. Write, then verify; never verify by assembly.
- A hedge stacked on a hedge ("with colleagues I helped") turns a true claim into none. Say what
  the reader needs and put the credit beside it.
- Rhythm is editorial evidence: read the passage aloud; breath, emphasis and the urge to paraphrase
  mark the sentence to change.

## 5. Working with the author

The author is the authority on every fact and every sentence, and the seat's job is to propose.
Both halves hold at once: nothing is settled without the author's word, and every finding reaches
the author as a proposed sentence, never as a question the seat could have answered by drafting.

- **The author's answers are facts, never copy.** When the author answers a question in chat, the
  seat extracts the facts and writes the reader's sentence in the surface's register. The author's
  words are quoted only where they wrote them as copy (an edit in the editor, a phrase sent with
  "use this"). The author's reasons about themselves are private even when they decide a public
  sentence: records carry the decision, never the reasoning about the self.
- **One draft at a time, never a menu.** A proposal is one sentence or one paragraph the author
  can accept, edit or refuse, with the fact it rests on named in a clause. Where the seat lacks a
  fact, it proposes from the facts it holds and names the gap.
- **A conversation before a change.** The seat brings the trade space and what it thinks best in
  context and why, and the draft does not move until the conversation settles a named list;
  applying happens only on that list. A limit (a field's characters, a count of links) is never a
  reason to cut; it is a reason to ask where else the content lives, since there is more than one
  way.
- **The author's instruments.** A table the author edits in place and a numbered reply; for a
  backlog, self-contained question cards, each carrying the text under decision, the reader's take
  and one proposal per option, answerable from the card alone; a diff of the seat's proposals
  against the author's last accepted version, that version preserved for rollback, shown where the
  author reads (a local editor, a browser page). A diff is read for what moved, never for whether
  the result reads: copy is judged aloud, as the person.
- **Every message leads with what changed that the author did not ask for.** Proposed copy goes
  short and at the top. A settled item is never listed again, not even as closed; an item the
  author calls their own edit gets no proposal; agreed intent is never critiqued for not existing
  yet; a fresh metaphor for an agreed item is the fluency trap, not a finding.
- **Never defer with "at your word", never cite the author's earlier words back as rules.** The
  author can change their mind, learn, and meet new circumstances; the question is what is best in
  context. A correction fixes the seat's model; the words are still chosen for the reader, not to
  match what the author said most vehemently.
- **Authority and fact.** An adjective true by the author's authority ("the framework is
  innovative, I invented it") or an accepted fact among the readers stands; the voice pitfall is
  for claims the reader would doubt. The author's figures about their own work are not checked
  against the web unless the author asks.
- **The live surface and the author's hand.** When the author edits the live surface in parallel,
  the repository copy follows the live state: a read-back precedes every write, and the
  repository holds a record of the surface, not its source.

## 6. Verify

- Facts and attribution against the sources: the particulars, the records, the public
  repositories. Names, dates, titles, numbers, licences and product names exactly as their owners
  spell them; a product's name consistent across every vendor's ecosystem that carries it.
- An award or honour goes on the page only in the status the awarding body's own record shows. A
  win and a nomination feel the same from the inside, so the body's page or the press release
  decides the verb, and a remembered win with no record is named to the author as unconfirmed,
  never written.
- An absolute claim about a field's history ("no framework existed then") is checked and, if
  contestable, written as the true meaning without the false absolute.
- Length is information, never a target. Measure once and state it with the draft; verify the
  surface's limits at the counter, never from memory. Density is a global property of the piece,
  never a local cut: if the surface's field refuses the text at application, the choice of what
  moves to another surface is editorial and the author's.

## 7. The readers, before the author

The readers in order, large decisions before small, as `editorial-strategy.md` §Review sequence
sets. Each is a falsifier; none is an authority. A reader's finding is information the seat
decides on, by the `review-feedback-defaults-to-triage` discipline: reject the incorrect with a
reason, absorb the correct and proportionate, name a home for the rest, and show the dispositions
with the draft.

1. **The voice check**, run by the seat: `editorial-voice` §The check, all five questions, plus
   the per-sentence test from step 3.
2. **`editor`**, the developmental pass: does the piece do the reader's job; structure, attention,
   evidence and attribution, register and voice. Revise on its letter before any sentence work.
3. **`prose-expert`**, the line pass: clarity, rhythm, word choice, redundancy, with its
   before-and-after rewrites inside the register. Revise again.
4. **`audience-reader`**, on the revised draft: give it items 1 to 4 of the reader's decision,
   built from the author instance and the private brief where one exists and never from a generic
   professional search, and the draft in its reading path, and withhold item 5 and the beliefs
   from step 3, so that what it reports believing is the draft's effect and not the brief's.
   Compare its belief with step 3 afterwards; the gap is the finding. The readers report beliefs,
   not inventory: an empty section, a date to reconstruct, two items that "look the same" are
   stock-taking and are struck in triage before the author reads.
5. **The proof pass**, the seat's own, against the sources (§9).

A sentence written to satisfy a reader's wish ("the papers stay private") is pandering to a
finding that was never a problem; every proposal regrounds in the intent and in editorial
excellence before it answers a reader.

## 8. Then the author

What reaches the author:

```text
Reader's decision: the five items of step 1
Beliefs the piece must leave: 1. 2. 3.
Draft: the text, field by field where the surface has fields
Length: characters, as information; the surface's limit and whether it is verified
Readers: voice check; prose-expert and editor dispositions; audience-reader's belief and noise
Proposals: one sentence each, the fact named, the gap named where the seat lacks a fact
```

The author's spoken reaction and direct edits are primary editorial evidence. A correction fixes
the model; the words are still chosen for the reader.

## 9. The three editing altitudes

When revising an existing piece, edit from large decisions to small, one altitude per pass,
never two in one pass. Polishing sentences inside a structure that has not earned its shape is
wasted work.

- **Developmental.** Does the piece do the reader's job? Read once without writing, then
  summarise in one paragraph what the piece actually is; compare that with the reader's decision
  and name the drift; name the structural changes in priority order and the one to make first.
  Leave sentences alone unless a sentence signals a structural fault. Output: an editorial letter.
- **Line.** Clarity first, rhythm second; concrete for abstract; vary sentence length; remove
  redundancy within sentences and across paragraphs; a fix that loses the voice is the wrong fix.
  Output: the edited draft and a change log by kind (clarity, rhythm, word choice, redundancy).
- **Proof.** The last pass: names, dates, titles, URLs, product names; consistency of capitals,
  hyphens and numerals; flag every ambiguity and never silently change a fact. Output: the
  corrected draft, the change log, the flagged ambiguities for the author to confirm.

`editor` takes the developmental pass and `prose-expert` the line pass, in that order; the proof
pass is the seat's own, run against the sources.

## 10. Repetition across a surface with many entry points

Readers of a profile enter by section and may never read the About, so an echo across sections
is not the same defect as a relist inside one. For any duplication, write each surface's intent
in one line before touching copy (the card proves the role; the project entry describes the
artefact; the About tells the story), then choose the sentence for the job. An identical opener
on two surfaces is an accident to trace to intent, never a thing to smooth away with a variant.
Repetition hides in nouns ("built ... built"); read aloud before the readers do.

## Gotchas

- A partition is not writing. A card cut by a date seam into two entries to meet a verified limit,
  the author's paragraphs sorted by period and "seam sentences" written to join them, puts labels
  where the story had been, halves the sentences whose meaning is their span, and lets the count
  choose where the seam falls. A sentence written to join two of the author's is copy and fires
  this skill from §1. When a field refuses a card, the arc stays whole and the how moves to the
  entries that warrant it, written for their reader.
- A true, verifiable fact is not a reason to include it. A fact that says nothing to the reader it
  is written for does not earn its place in the few words the surface allows.
- The summary section and the experience entry draw on the same facts. The entry particularises:
  who, when, what was decided, what it cost. It never restates the summary's sentences.
- "The function. With colleagues I helped grow ... : pushing, hiring, forming, arguing" is the
  shape that fails: a label, a colon, a litany of gerunds. A résumé, not a person.
- A tense that dates the text ("is passing to") is a choice to surface to the author, never to
  hide.
- Vendor terms for a product (plugin, connector, app) appear only when naming the vendor's
  listing; elsewhere the product's own name.
- A mis-cast reader's question answered in the open (who reported to whom, an org chart, who
  holds the budget) grades the author on that reader's axis. If the axis is wrong the question is
  not answered in copy.
- A verb chosen for strength can contradict the author's self-description: "set" where the author
  described enabling, guiding and holding space is the opposite of what they said. The verb is
  chosen with the author.
- A date the author does not want attached to a sensitive decision is left unattached; readability
  and the right context are the targets.
- The seat's reach for an external authority (a taxonomy, an earlier document, a recruiter's
  frame, a reviewer's wish) in place of the author's word and the understanding the piece carries
  pulls every draft toward the generic. The authority is the author; the sources are material.
